Picking an interface

triib works on one network interface at a time: pick it from the menu at the left of the toolbar, which shows each wired interface with its speed and whether it has a PTP hardware clock. Virtual interfaces stay hidden unless you ask for them. The status bar says how the interface is doing and the entity ID triib discovers as.

triib asks every entity to announce itself when it starts, and again whenever you press the refresh button. Each entity it finds has its entity model read once and kept, so a device you have seen before shows in full at once the next time.

On Linux triib needs CAP_NET_RAW, on macOS access to /dev/bpf*, and on Windows Npcap. The packages set the first two up; when something is missing, triib says what and how to fix it. On a Mac, triib reaches the Mac's own AVB entity through Apple's AudioVideoBridging framework, as macOS never hands that entity what a program writes to BPF.

Connections

The matrix has every talker output across the top and every listener input down the side, grouped by entity. Point at a cell to see its talker and listener and whether their formats meet; click it to connect or disconnect. An entity's row folds into one line, where a cell says how many of its streams are connected, and opens to connect them one by one.

A cell showsMeaning
ConnectedThe listener is bound and receiving
Bound, waiting for the streamThe listener is bound, but the talker's stream has not arrived yet
Bound, something is wrongBound, but the reservation failed or the formats differ; pointing at it says which
Can connectThe formats match: click to connect
Input format must change firstThe input takes the stream but is set to another format, so it may not play until its format changes
Formats cannot meetThe talker and listener have no format in common

Click a cell marked Input format must change first for a menu of the formats both ends take: pick one and triib changes the input, or both ends, and connects.

The chips above the matrix show all streams, only audio or only media clock streams, and Hide what cannot connect leaves out the rows and columns with nothing to connect. The search field in the toolbar finds entities and streams by name.

Entities

The entity list has a row for each entity: its name and group, product and firmware, Milan version, roles, media clock and more. The media clock's menu changes an entity's clock source right in the list. Each column header has a menu to move or remove its column or add another, such as vendor, model, state, AVB Lite or egress.

On Linux, Add talker, Add listener and Add device add the computer's own; see below.

Inspector

Select an entity, in the matrix, the entity list or the network, and the inspector shows all there is to know about it, in five tabs:

  • Entity: its name and group, which you can rename, its product, firmware and configuration, its media clock with the clock source and sampling rate to change, its AVB Lite and AVB Wireless state, its identity and roles. Identify makes the device show itself, as by a blinking light.
  • Streams: each stream's format, which you can change, its state and maximum transit time, and how the stream's channels map to the entity's audio.
  • Controls: the entity's controls, such as gain, volume and mute, to set.
  • Diagnostics: how each clock domain and stream has fared since the entity started, from the counters it keeps, with what went wrong marked, and how each bound input's reservation stands.
  • Descriptors: the entity model itself, descriptor by descriptor.

Network

The network view draws the network from each entity's gPTP path: bridges at the top, the devices behind each of them below, and those connected straight to the grandmaster's bridge in the middle. A panel beside the map counts what it shows and lists what needs attention. The chips switch what is drawn on the map:

  • Audio: each stream as its own wire, entering and leaving every bridge it crosses. Each talker has a hue and its streams are shades of it. Moving dots mean audio is flowing; a still red line is a failed reservation and a still gray line is advertised with no listener ready, both stopping where the reservation stops.
  • CRF: the media clock streams, the same way.
  • gPTP: the clock tree from the grandmaster, with each device in sync or not.

Click a wire to inspect its stream, or a device to see its streams; click the background to clear. On a narrow window the map and the panel take turns.

AVB Lite

AVB Lite gives AVB devices a working if degraded path over switches that are not AVB bridges: plain PTP end to end, and streams unicast to each listener. triib shows how each entity runs it:

  • The inspector's AVB Lite section gives the entity's mode and why it fell back, its PTP profile and offset from the grandmaster, its media VLAN, how many listeners it serves unicast, and its link speed.
  • When no AVB bridge is heard and entities run AVB Lite, the network view's clock chip reads PTP and draws the PTP tree. A device is in sync while it follows the grandmaster within 50 µs.
  • The alarms the profile calls for, such as a PTP offset past what AVB Lite allows, show in the status bar and the inspector.

AVB Wireless

The AVB Wireless profile carries AVB over one Wi-Fi hop: an access point that is a bridge on its wired port, and Wi-Fi stations as talkers and listeners. triib asks each entity's AVB interfaces for their wireless status and shows it:

  • The inspector's Wireless section gives a station's role, time mode and whether its time holds, its link, signal and rate, and the access point it follows; on an access point, its stations, the listeners it cannot serve and the frames a second it can deliver.
  • An access point admits Class A only where it delivers 12000 frames a second or more; triib-cli wireless-config allows it or keeps it off.
  • The network view draws each station under its access point, its Wi-Fi hop dotted, and in sync while its time is locked. A station not locked and listeners an access point cannot serve show as alarms.

Presets

A preset keeps each entity's clock sources, sampling rates, stream formats, controls and connections, and this computer's own talkers and listeners. Name it and save it from the toolbar's presets button; recalling it later changes only what differs, and says how many changes it made.

Log

The log lists every ATDECC frame triib sends and hears, newest first, each decoded into plain words with its time, direction and entity. A frame that breaks the rules is marked with the reason, and a response that refuses a command shows its status. Show all frames, only warnings, or only ADP, AECP or ACMP; pause the log to read it, or clear it.

This computer's talkers, listeners and devices

On Linux, triib can run talkers, listeners and devices of the computer's own, which other controllers see and bind like any device. They need:

  • A wired interface with a PTP hardware clock; ethtool -T <interface> shows whether it has one.
  • linuxptp's ptp4l running gPTP on that interface, so the clock keeps the network's time. triib reads its state and says when it is not there.

Add them with Add talker, Add listener and Add device in the entity list. A talker or a listener is a Milan entity with one stream; a device is one with any number of streams each way, which the inspector adds and removes. Each stream is AAF or AM824 at 48, 96 or 192 kHz, 8 channels unless the inspector picks another count, in SR class A or B.

A talker sends from one of the computer's audio devices and a listener plays to one, as the inspector picks. Each of a device's streams has a virtual audio device of its own in PipeWire, named after the device and the stream, such as Host device 1 Out 1, for any program to play into or record from, unless the inspector picks a sound card instead. triib-endpointd runs them all, and keeps the names, formats and bindings controllers give, so a listener binds again after a restart.

Where no AVB bridge answers on the interface, they fall back to AVB Lite. The Linux packages carry systemd units that move ptp4l to AVB Lite's PTP profile when they do, and back to gPTP when the link comes up again, and keep Energy-Efficient Ethernet and PAUSE off the link, as the profile asks. The README has the settings.

Settings

Settings has the language, any of 38 or the system's, and the appearance: light, dark or the system's, the colors, and whether triib animates. triib takes its colors from the system's accent color, or from the Omarchy theme, unless you pick one. Settings also has outside control: whether AI agents may control triib, and the agents you have allowed, which Forget clears.

AI agents

triib --mcp lets an AI agent such as Claude Code see and change your network through the triib on your desktop, over the Model Context Protocol; it starts triib when it is not running. To add it to Claude Code:

claude mcp add triib -- triib --mcp

The agent can read triib's status, the entities, each one's streams, formats and clocks, and the connections, and change what a click would: connect and disconnect streams, set a stream's format, a sampling rate or a clock source, rename and identify. A change answers once the device has, with what it said. The first time an agent connects, triib asks you whether to let it in.

Command line

triib-cli does what the app does from a terminal or a script. A few examples:

triib-cli interfaces
triib-cli discover enp2s0
triib-cli connect enp2s0 <talker-id>:0 <listener-id>:0
triib-cli rate enp2s0 <entity-id> 0 96000
triib-cli identify enp2s0 <entity-id>

Run it with no arguments for every command; the README lists them too.
